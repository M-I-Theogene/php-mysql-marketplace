1. Purpose and legal basis

Cypadi Ltd (Cypadi, we, us) is committed to preventing money laundering, terrorist financing, fraud and the abuse of its marketplace. This policy sets out how Cypadi identifies the parties it deals with, monitors transactions, settles with the vendors who sell on its platform, keeps records and reports suspicion.

This policy is adopted in line with Law No. 058/2023 of 2023 on the prevention and punishment of money laundering offences, the directives and guidance of the Financial Intelligence Centre (FIC) of Rwanda, and the requirements of the payment institution and mobile money operators through which Cypadi receives customer payments. Where a partner requirement is stricter than this policy, the stricter requirement applies.

2. Scope and business model

Cypadi operates shop.cypadi.com, an online marketplace in Rwanda. Independent sellers (vendors) list products on the platform; customers browse, order and pay in Rwandan francs by MTN Mobile Money or Airtel Money. Cypadi reviews each vendor before any product is published, collects the customer payment, and settles the vendor once the order has been completed.

This policy applies to every customer who registers on the platform, every vendor who applies to sell on it, and every employee, contractor and administrator who has access to customer data, orders or payment functions.

Cypadi does not operate a mobile money wallet or a bank account on behalf of a customer, does not take deposits, does not provide money transfer, currency exchange or lending services, and does not pay interest on any balance it holds. Mobile money PINs and wallet credentials are never requested, seen or stored by Cypadi. The only funds Cypadi holds are customer payments for confirmed orders, held in its settlement account with its payment partner until the order is completed or refunded under section 13.

4. Governance and responsibilities

Managing Director. The Managing Director is accountable for this policy, approves it, and ensures that Cypadi has the resources to apply it.

Compliance Officer. Cypadi appoints a Compliance Officer as its money laundering reporting officer. The Compliance Officer:

  • owns and maintains this policy and the Vendor Due Diligence Procedures;
  • reviews vendor applications, including the identity document supplied, and approves or rejects them before any product is published;
  • reviews monitoring alerts and decides whether a transaction is released, held, refunded or reported;
  • approves or withholds vendor settlements where an investigation is open;
  • files suspicious transaction reports with the FIC where required, and keeps the register of reports;
  • maintains the record of staff briefings and this policy review cycle.

5. Customer due diligence

5.1 Standard due diligence (all customers)

The following is collected from every customer and constitutes the standard CDD record:

  • at registration: full name, email address, phone number and username;
  • at checkout: delivery address, city, province, country, the items ordered, the order value, and the payment reference of the mobile money deposit.

This record is sufficient to identify the payer and to reconcile every order to a single payment. No product is dispatched unless the payment has been confirmed by the payment partner.

5.2 Enhanced due diligence (EDD)

Cypadi applies enhanced due diligence where the risk of the customer or of the transaction warrants it. EDD is not triggered by order value alone: Cypadi sells ordinary consumer goods, including high-value electronics, and a legitimate customer may spend a large sum on a single item. The value of an order is therefore one factor in the risk assessment under section 7, never a reason by itself to refuse or delay a sale.

Cypadi obtains a copy of a government-issued identity document (national identity card or passport), and records the source of funds where relevant, before completing a transaction where any of the following applies:

  • the name or phone number of the payer differs materially from the registered account holder;
  • the customer is a politically exposed person, a family member or a known close associate;
  • the transaction has been flagged under section 9;
  • the customer is rated high risk under section 7;
  • the transaction is unusual for the customer's established order pattern, including a sudden or unexplained jump in order size, frequency or delivery destination;
  • the payment originates from a number, device or location inconsistent with the account history.

Where a single order exceeds RWF 5,000,000, or cumulative purchases exceed RWF 10,000,000 within a rolling period of thirty (30) days, the Compliance Officer reviews the order before it is released for dispatch. That review is a control on the transaction, not a condition of the sale: an identified customer buying an expensive item is not refused, and the order proceeds once the review is complete.

Where satisfactory identification cannot be obtained, Cypadi cancels the order and refunds the payment to the original paying number through the same provider.

6. Vendor due diligence

Before any vendor product appears on shop.cypadi.com, Cypadi verifies the vendor identity and business details and a Compliance Officer approves the application. Until that approval is recorded in the platform, the vendor cannot publish any listing and cannot receive settlement. The detailed checks, the approval gate, the risk rating, the prohibited-product list and the decision record are set out in the Vendor Due Diligence Procedures (CYP-VDD-001), which is incorporated into this policy by reference.

7. Risk assessment

Customers, vendors and transactions are rated low, medium or high risk. The rating is recorded, determines the level of due diligence applied, and fixes the re-review interval below.

8. Sanctions, PEP and adverse media screening

At onboarding, and on the re-review interval set in section 7, Cypadi screens vendor owners and any high-value customer against:

  • the United Nations Security Council consolidated sanctions list;
  • the OFAC Specially Designated Nationals list of the United States Treasury;
  • the European Union consolidated sanctions list;
  • the Rwandan gazette of designated persons and entities;
  • adverse media reporting relating to financial crime.

The screening result, the lists consulted and the date of screening are recorded in the customer or vendor file. A confirmed match results in refusal of onboarding or termination of the relationship, freezing or reversal of the affected transaction, and a report to the FIC where required.

9. Transaction monitoring

Cypadi reviews transactions daily against the following indicators. Every alert is examined by the Compliance Officer within one business day of being raised.

  • three or more failed payment attempts followed by a successful one from the same number;
  • one phone number paying for three or more accounts with no apparent connection;
  • a single order at or within five per cent of the provider published maximum;
  • several orders to one delivery address paid from different numbers;
  • repeated order-and-cancel or order-and-refund patterns;
  • an order placed from a device or network inconsistent with the account history;
  • a vendor listing products unrelated to its declared business category;
  • a vendor requesting early settlement, or a change of settlement number shortly after approval.

Cypadi may delay, hold, decline or reverse any transaction, and withhold any settlement, while an investigation is open, and may suspend the account concerned.

10. Suspicious transaction reporting

Any member of staff who becomes aware of activity they know or suspect to be linked to money laundering or terrorist financing must report it to the Compliance Officer immediately and in writing. The Compliance Officer assesses the report, records the decision and its reasons, and where Cypadi knows or suspects money laundering or terrorist financing, files a suspicious transaction report with the FIC without delay.

Cypadi does not disclose to the customer, the vendor, or any third party that a report has been made or is contemplated. Reports and the register of reports are held by the Compliance Officer and are not stored with ordinary customer records.

11. Record keeping and retention

Cypadi retains identification data, identity-document images, order and deposit records, vendor files, screening results, monitoring alerts, investigation files, settlement ledgers and reports for five (5) years after the end of the customer or vendor relationship, or for such longer period as the FIC or a competent authority directs. Records are stored on access-controlled systems, identity documents are served only through an authenticated route and never as public files, and records are produced to the payment partner, the mobile money operators, the FIC or any competent authority on request.

12. Third-party payment providers

Customer payments are collected in Rwandan francs through PawaPay Ltd, acting for the licensed mobile money operators MTN Mobile Money Rwanda and Airtel Money Rwanda. Cypadi relies on these regulated partners for the execution of the payment and for the settlement of collected funds into the Cypadi settlement account. Cypadi remains responsible for the identity of its customers and vendors, for the legitimacy of the orders it accepts, and for the settlement obligations it owes to approved vendors under section 13.

13. Vendor settlements and funds held

Cypadi is a marketplace operator, not a payment institution. It does not accept deposits, does not hold funds on behalf of customers beyond a confirmed order, and does not pay interest on any balance. The settlement rule below is binding and is applied to every vendor:

  • What is held. When a customer pays for an order, the payment is collected through PawaPay into the Cypadi settlement account. That money is held only for the purpose of completing or refunding that order.
  • When the vendor is paid. Cypadi settles an approved vendor within three (3) working days after the customer confirms delivery of the order.
  • Where the money comes from. Settlement is paid from Cypadi's own settlement account, by mobile money to the settlement number recorded in the vendor file at the time of approval.
  • What is paid. The product price of the completed order, less the commission stated in the Vendor Agreement. Delivery charges are not included in the vendor share.
  • Where delivery is not confirmed. If the customer does not confirm delivery, the order is treated as completed and enters the settlement run seven (7) days after the delivery record is created on the platform, unless the customer has raised a dispute.
  • When settlement is withheld. Cypadi withholds settlement where the order has been cancelled, refunded or disputed, where an identity or monitoring alert under section 9 is open, where the vendor has been suspended, or where a report to the FIC has been made. The vendor is told that settlement is on hold, but not the ground for a report.
  • Change of settlement number. A request to change the settlement number is treated as a high-risk event: it requires the Compliance Officer's approval, is confirmed in writing with the vendor, and is logged.

Cypadi keeps a settlement ledger recording, for each order, the amount collected, the commission applied, the amount released, the date released and the mobile money transaction reference. The ledger is reconciled against the payment partner statement at least once a month.

14. Refusal, suspension and termination

Cypadi may decline to onboard a customer or vendor, refuse or cancel an order, suspend or close an account, withhold a settlement, restrict access to the platform, or terminate a relationship where this policy or the Vendor Due Diligence Procedures are not met, where identification cannot be completed, or where suspicion of financial crime arises. The decision, the reason and the date are recorded. Where a payment has already been collected and the order cannot proceed, the payment is refunded to the original paying number.

15. Personal data protection

Personal data collected under this policy is processed only for the purposes set out in the Cypadi Privacy Policy, in line with Law No. 058/2021 of 2021 relating to the protection of personal data and privacy. A customer or vendor may request access to, correction of, or deletion of their personal data by writing to compliance@cypadi.com; requests are answered within thirty (30) days. A deletion request does not override the retention obligation in section 11.

16. Staff training and awareness

Every member of staff, contractor and administrator with access to customer data, orders or payments is briefed on this policy at induction and at least annually thereafter, and after any material amendment. The briefing covers identification requirements, the monitoring indicators in section 9, the settlement rule in section 13, the escalation route to the Compliance Officer and the prohibition on tipping off. Attendance is recorded and signed by the Compliance Officer.

17. Review and amendment

This policy is reviewed at least every twelve (12) months, and immediately on any material change in the Cypadi business model, product range, payment providers or applicable law, or following any serious compliance incident. Amendments are recorded in the version history table, approved by the Managing Director and issued as a new version number.
